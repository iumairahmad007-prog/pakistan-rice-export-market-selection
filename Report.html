1.
Executive summary
A Pakistani rice exporter with limited time and resources needs a disciplined way to decide
which foreign markets deserve further investigation. Import volume alone cannot answer that
question: a large market may have slow physical growth, established suppliers, unsuitable
product demand or difficult access. A sharp one-year increase may instead reflect a small
starting base, exceptional shipments or reporting gaps.
This project combines preserved UN Comtrade records with World Bank economic context and
separate Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (PBS) Basmati evidence. The common historical year is
2023. Quantity analysis covers 2018-2023; market size and pooled unit value use 2021-2023.
A conservative main panel contains 69 markets with complete positive, unestimated
quantities. Another 65 markets enter a separately labelled 134-market sensitivity universe.
Missing observations remain missing, and importer and exporter reports are kept separate.
The transparent base screen assigns 40% to market size, 30% to physical growth, 20% to
stability and 10% to pooled import unit value. Percentile normalization limits the influence of
highly skewed raw magnitudes. These weights express business judgment. The United States
scores highest. Azerbaijan and Oman genuinely tie at rank 6, but their ranges across five
weighting scenarios differ: 6-10 for Azerbaijan and 6-25 for Oman.
Commercial research priorities are a separate interpretation of the screen, supplier structure,
Pakistan's position, Basmati evidence, dated access research and practical uncertainties. Tier 1
comprises Oman and Azerbaijan. Tier 2 comprises Kenya, the United Kingdom, the United
States and Australia. Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan form a data-limited watchlist; Malaysia, South
Africa and Türkiye remain reserves. There is no numerical ordering within these groups.
Oman's stronger direct Basmati evidence can matter more to the business case than
Azerbaijan's narrower rank range.
HS 100630 measures broad semi-/wholly milled rice, not exact demand for 1121 or 1509
Basmati. Unit values are not selling prices or margins; supplier countries are not buyer
companies. Kenya demonstrates that growing total imports can coexist with a falling Pakistan
share, while Kazakhstan's large mirror-data gap limits bilateral confidence. The UK remains
commercially visible despite missing World quantities. Forecasting is deliberately excluded
because the short annual series cannot support robust validation here. The result is a
reproducible research framework, not a prediction of sales, profitability or successful entry.
2.
Business problem
The motivation comes from international business development around Pakistani rice exports.
Time spent contacting buyers, investigating compliance or arranging market visits has an
opportunity cost. Headline import value, a single year's growth or Pakistan's existing share is
insufficient on its own. Physical trends, volatility, supplier competition, product fit, access,
logistics and data confidence all affect the next research decision. This project makes the initial
screen explicit and reproducible while leaving buyer qualification and shipment economics for
subsequent commercial work.

Forecasting decision and dashboard
Forecasting was intentionally excluded
The preserved project data are annual only. The common quantity window supplies six annual
levels and five growth rates. Longer focus-market histories provide at most nine annual
observations through 2023 and eleven through 2025, with fewer usable observations for some
markets and incomplete recent global reporting.
The agreed preference for approximately
60 consistent monthly observations or 24
quarterly observations
was not met. These would be feasibility gates rather than
guarantees of forecast quality. Even the longest annual series leaves little room for repeated
out-of-sample validation in this project. No ARIMA, machine learning, artificial monthly series,
interpolation or CAGR extrapolation was added. Historical trends and sensitivity analysis are
used without presenting them as forecasts. This is a methodological decision, not unfinished
analysis.

Limitations and conclusion
Product scope and commercial measurement.
HS 100630 includes non-Basmati rice and
cannot measure exact 1121/1509 demand or buyer specifications. Import values are nominal,
and pooled unit values reflect product mix and valuation rather than achievable prices, quality
grades or margins. Total imports are not unmet demand or a sales pipeline.
Historical coverage and selection.
The common year remains 2023 because 2024/2025
global reporting continuity was incomplete. A 2026 source review does not make 2023 demand
current. The strict panel favors markets with complete, unestimated quantity reporting and is
not a representative sample of all importing economies. Official estimates remain explicit in
the extension.
Quantity reliability and missingness.
Missing UK World net weights prevent quantity-based
scoring. A missing row is not zero trade. No imputation was performed, and unusual source
observations were retained with flags. Botswana, Malawi, Mauritius, Tanzania and other review
cases show that satisfying the strict rule does not establish error-free demand measurement.
Percentiles bound numerical influence but do not repair source reliability.
Supplier and mirror interpretation.
Supplier-country data identify economies and reporting
areas, not companies, brands or buyers. World totals and special categories require explicit
treatment. Importer/exporter differences can be material; no averaging, reconciliation or
substitution was used. No causal explanation is established for Kenya's supplier shift or
Kazakhstan's mirror gap.
Model assumptions.
The 40/30/20/10 weights are subjective. A high relative score is not a
measured probability of success. Rank robustness covers the specified scenarios only, not
every possible weight choice. Scores normalized over 69, 67 and 134 markets are not
interchangeable. Economic context and Pakistan's position are interpreted separately rather
than forced into an additional composite.
Commercial execution.
No buyer-level demand evidence, freight-cost model, payment-risk
verification, achievable-margin calculation or sales/profit forecast was produced. Tariffs and
regulations can change; exact product classification, origin entitlement, documentation and
port-specific requirements need current verification. Commercial research priorities require
buyer qualification and consignment-level due diligence. No generated sales, company
strategy change or business impact is claimed.
What the project contributes
A useful selection process asks more than which country imports the most rice. It examines
market scale, changes in physical demand, the stability of those changes, existing suppliers,
Pakistan's position, direct Basmati evidence, practical access and the reliability of the
supporting observations. Here, the historical screen narrows the statistical comparison, while
commercial interpretation directs the next research effort. Keeping those two stages separate
makes both the assumptions and the unresolved questions visible.
